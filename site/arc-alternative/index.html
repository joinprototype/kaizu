Kaizu by Prototype

The Arc alternative for your Mac.

Arc stops working on December 22. Kaizu keeps the sidebar you’re used to, and it’s free.

Download for Mac

Free and open source. For Macs with Apple chips, on macOS 15.4 or later.

What gets better

  • Ads, trackers and YouTube ads are blocked, with no extension needed.
  • Cookie pop-ups get rejected for you.
  • It runs on the same engine as Safari, so it stays light on your Mac.
  • There’s no account to close. Your tabs and history stay on your Mac, and the code is public.

How to move

Do it before December 22. After that, Arc says accounts close and their data is removed within 30 days.

  1. In Arc, open Settings, choose Advanced, then Export Data. You get a list of your links.
  2. Download Kaizu and follow the steps for opening it the first time.
  3. Press ⌘T in Kaizu, paste a link you want to keep, and pin it to your sidebar. Repeat for the rest.
  4. Add your extensions. Open one on the Chrome Web Store and press Add to Kaizu.
  5. Move any passwords saved in Arc to a password manager, since the list of links won’t bring them. Then sign in to your sites once.
  6. Make Kaizu your default browser in Settings, under General.

Good to know

Does Kaizu read my Arc data?

No. Use Arc’s export and the steps above.

Will my sites work?

Kaizu uses Safari’s engine, so sites work the way they do in Safari. Many Chrome extensions work too, but some need features only Chrome has.

What isn’t in Kaizu?

Sync between devices, a phone app, Easels, Notes and Boosts. Passkey sign-in for Google doesn’t work yet, so use your password there.

Does it work on Windows?

No. Kaizu is made for the Mac.
